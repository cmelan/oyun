VERDICT ON THE BUILD — unsparing. The master plan's own one-line diagnosis is correct and I can confirm it from the code and the screenshots: this is an award-quality vertical slice followed by nine copies of a placeholder. The entrance screen and Level 1 (painted plates, parallax, the dormant→awake oak crossfade, the cooperation finale) would compete credibly. Everything after it would not. After the M1 scenery pass, levels 2–10 have gained real horizons and depth — a genuine improvement over the two-rows-of-ellipses baseline — but they still read as flat vector programmer art with a painted sprite standing in them, and the style collision is now more visible, not less. L10-3 is the proof: the FINAL boss of the game is still a green rounded rectangle with two white dots and four pink circles on top. One creature sprite serves all ten biomes. Nine of ten levels display the identical objective "Follow the glowing path →" and no glowing path is rendered anywhere. All ten biomes play one 8-note pentatonic loop. Level names and every intro hint for levels 2–10 are hardcoded Turkish, so an English-language judge sees "Bölüm 4 · Kestane Korusu" and Turkish instruction text. And the learning loop — the product's central claim — does not survive inspection: bark clues are byte-identical for two of three species in two regions (literally unanswerable), distractors are always the same three species, wrong answers are free and unlimited, every species is quizzed exactly once, and Level 3 teaches a child that olive trees live in caves.

What is genuinely strong and should be protected: the non-violence thesis is real and mechanically expressed, not pasted on — that is the Peace Prize case and it is the best asset here. The purchase boundary is exemplary: one non-consumable, grown-up-gated, web demo fully unlocked, no ads, no subscription, no energy, no timers, honest privacy policy. Engineering health is good — 110 tests green, clean build, 137 kB bundle, 60 fps, zero console errors.

STRATEGIC READ: the biggest risk is not craft, it is that nothing has shipped. There is no App Store record, no RevenueCat product, no video, no promo code, no TestFlight build — and store presence is a pass/fail intake gate, so a perfect game that misses it scores zero in all three categories. Two concrete blockers named in the repo remain open: the account spend limit that killed multi-agent execution, and the fact that authored art for biomes 2–10 cannot be produced by an agent. Priority order for the next 60 days: (1) ship to the App Store early and iterate — everything else is worthless without it; (2) cut the demo video, because two prescreeners watch exactly 120 seconds and Level 1 is already good enough to fill them; (3) localise the nine Turkish levels and give every level its own objective line — cheapest credibility win available; (4) fix the boss silhouettes and the creature roster, because "rounded rectangle with two dots" is the single image most likely to end a judge's goodwill; (5) make the learning loop honest, because the Peace Prize case rests on it. Also correct the two overclaims before a judge downloads the app: the Devpost copy promises an "adaptive musical layer" that does not exist, and one frameless screenshot (docs/submission/media/shipaton-1179x2556.png) carries an alpha channel that App Store Connect will reject.

## Current state, per criterion

### ELIGIBILITY — Live store presence inside the 1 Aug–30 Sep 2026 window — 0/10 → 10/10

Nothing is published. No App Store Connect record is evidenced anywhere in the repo. ios/ is a valid Capacitor shell with the right bundle id (com.cagatay.cokkalplikoruyucu, MARKETING_VERSION 1.0, landscape-locked, CODE_SIGN_STYLE Automatic) but no DEVELOPMENT_TEAM is set, so it has never been archived or signed. The only live build is a web demo at oyun-xovq.onrender.com, which does not satisfy this gate. This single row can zero out every other score in the table.

### ELIGIBILITY — RevenueCat SDK powering a real in-app purchase — 2/10 → 10/10

Code-side the boundary is clean and the best-tested part of the repo: src/game/purchases.ts configures the native SDK, checks the 'full_journey' entitlement, and hasFullJourney() returns true unconditionally on web so the demo stays judgeable; tests/purchases.test.ts covers key selection and entitlement parsing. But nothing exists on the dashboard side. .env.example still holds placeholder keys. presentFamilyPurchase() calls RevenueCatUI.presentPaywallIfNeeded, which requires a paywall configured in the RevenueCat dashboard — with no offering and no paywall it will silently return false and the child sees the menu again with no explanation. There is also no restore-purchases path anywhere in the UI, which Apple requires for non-consumables.

### ELIGIBILITY — Demo video, max 2 minutes, publicly hosted — 0/10 → 9/10

Does not exist. The script in docs/SHIPATON_2026.md is genuinely good: 1:42–1:50, six beats, correctly front-loads identity and the empathy loop, correctly names the three target awards on screen. It also correctly reflects the 2026 two-minute ceiling. But it is a plan, not a file — and note the script draws almost entirely on Level 1 and the entrance screen, which is legitimate but means the video will oversell the other nine levels unless they improve.

### ELIGIBILITY — Judge access: free trial or promo code — 0/10 → 10/10

Nothing generated. The design is judge-friendly by construction — the free Meadow chapter is complete and the hosted web build is fully unlocked — but the store build gates chapters 2–10 behind the non-consumable, so without codes a judge who downloads the app sees exactly one level and the paywall.

### ELIGIBILITY — Devpost registration, category answers and on-time submission — 0/10 → 10/10

No evidence of Devpost registration in the repo. The three prepared category answers in docs/SHIPATON_2026.md are well written and correctly scoped to Best Game, Design and Peace Prize. The decision not to chase the growth-based Grand Prize or #BuildInPublic is strategically correct and matches official advice against spraying categories. Risk: those answers currently overclaim (see the written-submission row).

### ELIGIBILITY — Apple Kids Category and age rating decision — 0/10 → 8/10

Never addressed in any document. RevenueCat is a third-party SDK receiving an anonymous app user id, device/OS info and purchase data — the privacy policy discloses this honestly, but no one has decided whether that is compatible with the Kids Category as Apple applies it. The in-app grown-up gate exists (a fixed arithmetic question with answers 12/15/18, correct 15) but is weak: three buttons and one correct answer is guessable by a child in three taps, and the answer never varies.

### BEST GAME — Gameplay depth, variety and content beyond the first level — 3/10 → 7/10

The weakest substantive row. Level 1 has a bespoke chapter, a cooperation finale and a restoration payoff. Levels 4–10 are literally the same level: all seven are one-line makeSection() recipes sharing SECTION_RHYTHM and a fixed 140px gap, producing 8 platforms, 3 trees, 3 puzzles, 3 monsters and widths differing only by a tier term. Levels 5 and 6 are structurally identical. Six puzzle archetypes are recycled across all ten chapters, several duplicated inside a single level (L2 has grow twice and thorn twice; L3 has rock, torch and mush each twice). One creature sprite — the Meadow's painted Mossling — is every creature in the game, appearing in a crystal cave and on a Mediterranean coast. The empathy loop, the game's core verb, is OPTIONAL outside Level 1: monsters can be jumped or tanked, and healing pays a heart and nothing else.

### BEST GAME — Game feel, difficulty curve and failure handling for ages 5–8 — 3/10 → 7/10

Actively unkind in ways the audit proves with code. Losing the third heart discards the entire level: onRetry rebuilds the scene, resetting respawn to spawn, every interact.done, every checkpoint and the boss — so a child who dies at the boss of a 4,400px level redoes every puzzle. The 'invisible assist' that is supposed to help strugglers can mathematically never fire: assistFactors only acts when deaths > 2, but the player has 3 hearts and game-over triggers first, and create() resets deaths to 0 on every retry — it is unit-tested only at deaths:20, a state normal play cannot reach. Difficulty is flat: level 5 is no harder than level 4 (+10 spd, +12 aggro), and the shrink finisher makes bosses progressively easier as their hitbox shrinks, so the difficulty curve runs backwards. Level 1 — the flagship — contains a soft-lock: the grow puzzle at x=1450 is hard-gated on a healed helper behind a 205px gap against a ~198px maximum jump, recoverable only by a 12-second no-progress rescue timeout.

### DESIGN AWARD — Aesthetics, visual craft and stylistic consistency — 4/10 → 7/10

Bimodal, and the gap is the problem. The entrance screen (painted ancient oak, warm light, real depth) and Level 1 are genuinely competitive — the dormant→awake oak crossfade is the best image in the project. Everything else is not. After the M1 scenery pass, levels 2–10 gained noise-based ridge silhouettes, cloud bands, a celestial body and platform surface patterns — a real, measurable improvement over the two-rows-of-ellipses baseline. But they still read as flat vector art: L02 platforms are grey brick that reads as castle wall on a mountain; L06's 'orchard' has no blossom, no fruit, no rows, and its sleeping trees render grey-blue in the one biome whose identity is blossom; L10's savanna is generic. Worse, the scenery pass made the style collision MORE visible, not less: a hand-painted Guardian and a hand-painted Mossling now stand in front of competent procedural scenery drawn in an entirely different visual language. Learning trees remain flat vector cartoons with two white googly eyes standing in painted landscapes — they read as stickers.

### DESIGN AWARD — Innovation, signature interactions and animation — 4/10 → 7/10

There are three genuinely good pieces of craft and they are all under-exploited. The dormant→awake oak crossfade (smoothstep over 2.45s) is excellent and is the only moment where art, audio and story fire together. The shrink finisher — the boss visibly ends the fight tiny and harmless, a perfect expression of the non-violence thesis — is the best boss beat in the game and nothing in the level, the UI or the docs draws attention to it. The soft-edged radial lantern in the cave is tastefully rendered. Against that: every boss in the game including the FINAL boss is a rounded rectangle with two white dots (L10-3 confirms this is still true after the scenery pass); the mimic's 'leafy disguise' is four flat circles on a rectangle, so the reveal has nothing to reveal; and UI chrome leans on emoji as art. Restoration — the emotional payoff the whole game is built toward — happens only in Level 1; the other nine end with particles and a DOM card and an unchanged world.

### PEACE PRIZE — Impact: clarity of the social benefit — 6/10 → 9/10

The strongest asset in the project and the most credible prize. The thesis — frightened behaviour is not bad character — is specific, teachable to a five-year-old, and expressed as a mechanic rather than a message: the child cannot defeat the Mossling, only calm it, heal it, and then depend on it to open the path. That is a real design achievement and it is rare in the category. The honest caveat is that it is fully delivered exactly once. In levels 2–10 the empathy loop is optional — monsters can be jumped, healing pays a heart and a healed creature just bobs in place because the follow/cooperate behaviour is gated behind idx===0. So a judge who plays past Level 1 sees the thesis quietly stop being true.

### PEACE PRIZE — Feasibility and reach — 5/10 → 8/10

Structurally sound, currently unproven. Offline-first, no account, no network dependency, 137 kB bundle, three languages, and a complete free chapter — all genuine feasibility arguments for family and classroom reach, and unusually strong ones. Undercut by three things: nothing is shipped, so reach today is zero; the Turkish-first content is not actually localised past the UI chrome, so the multilingual reach claim is partly untrue; and landscape-only with no portrait layout narrows one-handed and accessibility contexts.

### PRODUCT CLAIM — Tree-recognition learning loop integrity — 2/10 → 7/10

The central claim of the product and it does not survive inspection. Bark clues are byte-identical for two of three species in two of three bark regions — drawBarkSwatch groups çam/servi/toros sediri/ardıç/ladin into one hard-coded branch, so in Toros the question 'which tree has this bark?' has two indistinguishable correct-looking answers and is pure luck. Distractors never vary: seven of ten regions have exactly three species, so pick3 always shows the same three. Wrong answers cost nothing and the card stays open for unlimited retries, so expected taps to success is 2 and nothing requires looking at the clue. Every species is quizzed exactly once and never revisited; species appearing in two regions are silently skipped the second time; level 10 reviews the six most RECENTLY learned species, precisely the ones needing review least. LEAF_PATH has only 8 species paths and falls back to oak, so kayın and kavak are both drawn as oak leaves while the game's own data says kavak is triangle-leaved — the child is asked to identify a leaf that is drawn wrong. And Level 3 teaches willow and olive inside a cave, a false habitat association delivered to five-year-olds by the one system the game exists for.

### CRAFT — Audio and music identity — 2/10 → 6/10

The weakest system in the codebase and a live submission-claim risk. There are three musical moods for ten biomes and only two are reachable: startLevel() sets 'meadow' unconditionally for every level, and 'restored' is gated on idx===0. So a crystal cave and a mountain summit are scored with identical 8-note pentatonic loops at identical 430ms pace — bit-identical audio for the two most visually distant scenes in the game. There is no ambience bed anywhere: a painted stream in Level 1 makes no sound. setMusicOn() is exported and called from nowhere. The AudioContext is resumed exactly once at construction with an unhandled promise, while a self-rescheduling setTimeout chain keeps scheduling notes against a frozen currentTime when iOS suspends — a minute backgrounded queues roughly 140 note-pairs that all detonate at once on resume, into a five-year-old's ears, with no limiter. And the Capacitor shell never sets AVAudioSession, so the default Ambient category means a phone with the ringer switch off plays nothing at all — silently disabling the game's entire pre-reader instruction channel, since every recognition card is audio-first by design. That is the most likely single-device demo failure.

### CRAFT — Wordless play and pre-reader accessibility — 3/10 → 7/10

The main menu is entirely text — 'New Journey', 'Continue', 'Journey', 'Nature Journal', 'How to Play' — so the stated audience cannot operate the front door of the game. The objective bar communicates a seven-step Level 1 script in emoji that uses 💛 twice for different things and 🌿/🌀 for two different puzzles; a five-year-old cannot map that row onto a place in the world. The hint bar is text-only and never spoken, even though speak() already exists and reading tree names aloud on the recognition card is the single best teaching decision in the codebase. Powers are distinguished by colour alone across five eyes including red and green, which is a functional block for colour-blind children, not a cosmetic one. Nine of ten levels tell the child in text to 'Follow the glowing path' and no glowing path is rendered. Landscape-only.

### CRAFT — Localisation completeness (TR/EN/DE) — 4/10 → 9/10

Split. The i18n dictionary is genuinely complete and well maintained — I verified 93 keys in each of tr, en and de with zero missing on either side, which is better than most hackathon entries. But the level content bypasses it entirely: every level name ('Bölüm 4 · Kestane Korusu') and every intro and hint string for levels 2–10 is a hardcoded Turkish literal inside src/core/levels.ts. So an English-language judge who plays past Level 1 gets Turkish chapter titles and Turkish instruction text for nine of ten chapters — including the only line of teaching some levels have. Given the target categories are Best Game and Design, this reads as abandonment rather than as a Turkish-first product decision.

### CRAFT — Stability, performance and build health — 8/10 → 9/10

The strongest engineering row and genuinely good for a hackathon entry. 110 tests pass across 10 files in about 2 seconds. Build and typecheck are green. Bundle is 137 kB (47 kB gzip). A ten-level automated sweep reports 60.2 fps at 960×540 with zero console errors. The scenery system shipped with its own test file and held the frame budget. Two real risks remain, both named in the audit: decoded raster memory is flagged as the iPhone SE blocker, and there is no view-frustum culling, no image decode step, and an unbounded particle array — none of which the headless 960×540 harness will surface.

### CRAFT — Child safety and freedom from dark patterns — 9/10 → 10/10

Close to exemplary and worth defending loudly. One non-consumable lifetime unlock; no subscription, no ads, no energy system, no artificial scarcity, no streaks, no timers, no child-facing upsell. The paywall is reachable only through a grown-up check. Progress and settings are local-only; the privacy policy at public/privacy.html is honest and specific about exactly what RevenueCat receives and does not overclaim. The free chapter is complete rather than a truncated teaser. Two small gaps: the grown-up gate is a single fixed arithmetic question with three buttons and one constant answer, guessable in three taps by the child it is meant to exclude; and the purchase-entry button appears in the child's main menu rather than only in a settings context.

### SUBMISSION — Written description and category answers — 6/10 → 8/10

Well written and correctly scoped — the three prepared answers in docs/SHIPATON_2026.md are among the better artifacts in the repo, and the decision not to dilute across categories matches official guidance. But two claims are currently false against the build and both are checkable in 30 seconds. The Best Game answer says 'Every action changes animation, colour, particles, and an adaptive musical layer'; the Design answer says 'adaptive sound'. The build has one 8-note loop for all ten biomes and exactly one music state change in the entire game, gated to Level 1. The Design answer also says the interface 'preserves one cohesive warm, botanical identity' — the screenshots do not support that across ten levels. The audit flags the audio claim explicitly as a submission-claim risk, and it is right.

### SUBMISSION — Store media: 1024px icon and frameless 1179×2556 screenshot — 7/10 → 9/10

Nearly done, with one concrete defect. public/icons/icon-1024.png is 1024×1024 with no alpha channel — correct for both Devpost and App Store Connect. docs/submission/media/shipaton-1179x2556.png is exactly the required dimensions but carries an RGBA alpha channel, which App Store Connect rejects on upload (Devpost will accept it). Three supporting stills exist (01-entrance, 02-oak-dormant, 03-oak-awake) and npm run submission:media regenerates them. All of them are drawn from the entrance screen and Level 1 — defensible as showing the best work, but it means the store listing shows nothing of nine-tenths of the game.

### SUBMISSION — Public repository and LICENSE — 10/10 → 10/10

Already satisfied and slightly over-invested. github.com/cmelan/oyun is PUBLIC with an MIT LICENSE, last pushed 27 Jul 2026. A clean clone runs npm ci && npm test && npm run build green. The repo also carries an unusually honest audit trail — 111 findings, a level matrix, before/after evidence — which reads well to anyone who looks. The one problem is that docs/SHIPATON_2026.md lists this as an eligibility gate, which is factually wrong and could cause effort to be spent defending a requirement that does not exist.

### SUBMISSION — Privacy policy and App Store Connect privacy disclosure — 6/10 → 9/10

The document itself is done and better than most: public/privacy.html is written, specific, honest about exactly what RevenueCat receives (anonymous app user id, device/OS info, receipt), and correctly states there is no account, no ads, no location and no behavioural analytics. It is dated 26 July 2026. The gap is entirely on the store side — the App Store Connect privacy disclosure has not been filed, and the intended public URL (oyun-xovq.onrender.com/privacy.html) has not been verified live from a signed-out session.

### STRATEGY — Category targeting and prizes deliberately not pursued — 7/10 → 8/10

The strategy is sound and I would not change it. Best Game, Design and Peace Prize is the right three: all exist in 2026 at $15k/$10k/$5k each, and the product has a real claim in each. Correctly declined: the Grand Prize ($100k) is awarded on post-launch user traction and growth momentum, which a just-shipped children's game with no marketing cannot win; HAMM rewards monetisation sophistication that would conflict with the child-safety stance; Catvertising requires RevenueCat Ads, which would be actively wrong here; Next Gen is students-only. #BuildInPublic has had zero activity and would now require two months of retroactive posting. One opportunity the repo has not considered: Samsung's Best App for Galaxy is a separate prize with a far smaller field, and the Galaxy Store is an accepted 2026 store — but it means a second platform build and should not compete with iOS shipping.

### EXECUTION — Capacity, timeline and the two named blockers — 3/10 → 8/10

Two blockers are recorded in the repo and both are live. First, the account spend limit hit on 2026-07-30 mid-audit killed 5 of 11 agents including all three research agents and the synthesis — multi-agent execution is unavailable until it is raised, and the master plan lists this as an active blocker. Second, authored art for biomes 2–10 cannot be produced by an agent at all (D1), so the ceiling on visual quality for nine of ten levels is procedural unless a human commissions plates. Against that, roughly nine weeks remain and the MUST milestone set (M1–M6) is scoped as self-contained and mostly unstarted: M1 has had one pass, M2–M6 have not begun. The critical path is not craft, it is App Store review.
