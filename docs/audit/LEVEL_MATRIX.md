One row per level. Rows 1–6 were produced by the level-design auditors; rows 7–10
were written by the lead after the audit run was cut short by an account spend limit
(see `docs/AUTONOMOUS_PROGRESS.md`).

## Level 1 — Bölüm 1 · İlk Adımlar (Çayır Vadisi)

`biome: meadow — the only level with authored raster art: painted far plate, parallax treeline, 3 foreground foliage clusters, 128px mirrored soil tile, 202px grass edge, painted Guardian + Mossling, dormant→awake Ancient Oak crossfade. w=3120, 6 platforms, gentle=true, no boss.` · priority **P1** · effort **M** · risk **medium**

**Intended emotion.** Arriving somewhere quiet that is hurt and does not blame you. Wonder → tenderness → the specific pride of having been kind. The child should leave believing the kindness was the thing that worked, not the jumping.

**Learning objective.** Three leaf-tier tree names (çınar / ıhlamur / meşe) plus the game's core grammar: a frightened creature is not a bad creature. Currently the botany and the empathy loop are taught in parallel but never touch each other — you can name three trees without ever understanding that the trees and the creature belong to the same living place. The level should be teaching one idea: *kindness gives you reach* (empathy is a traversal verb, not a side quest).

**Current mechanic.** Proximity auto-equip of 3 of the 5 eyes (❄️ freeze the stream, 🌿 grow leaf-steps, 🌀 wake a hanging bridge), each used exactly once; sand-throw → blind → hold-heal → happy on a single Mossling; a bespoke finale where the healed Mossling walks to a twin pressure stone at x=2538 while the Guardian stands on its own at x=2420, opening a root gate (solid at x=2640,w=34); then a 7-step objective bar, an Ancient Oak recognition card, and a 4.1s non-interactive restoration choreography with a music mood change.

**Repeated content.** All three puzzles are PUZZLE_FACTORY archetypes (freeze/grow/bridge) that the child will meet again in L2 (within 2000px), L4, L5, L6, L7 and L9 — identical geometry, identical solution, different colour. The single Mossling here is the *only* creature in all 10 levels. The checkpoint flag, the sleeping-tree beacon and the leaf-step slabs are drawn by the same code that draws them everywhere else. Nothing here is structurally unique except the companion finale — which is 300px long out of 3120.

**Difficulty & pacing.** gentle=true (falls teleport, cost nothing), 1 monster at spd 82 / aggro 185 (vs engine default 130/240), 3 hearts. Real hazard: sandMax = 1 monster + 0 boss + 2 buffer = 3, and sand only refills at checkpoints 560 / 1125 / 2080. The grow puzzle at x=1450 is hard-gated on already having a healed helper (LevelScene.doUse line ~243), and the 1490→1695 gap is 205px against a ~198px maximum jump — so it is genuinely impassable. A child who throws all 3 sand and misses is stuck behind a mandatory puzzle with no sand, recoverable only by the 12-second no-progress rescue flower (rescueToSafety grants exactly 1 sand). That is a soft-lock mitigated by a timeout, in the flagship level.

**Visual strengths.** Genuinely award-quality background craft: warm morning light, real atmospheric depth, hand-painted Guardian and Mossling with readable silhouettes, and the dormant→awake oak crossfade (a smoothstep reveal over 2.45s) is the single best image in the project. Foreground foliage occluding the player sells depth. The gold-particle restoration wash plus the 'restored' music mood is the only moment in the game where audio, art and story fire together.

**Visual weaknesses.** Style collision: the learning trees are flat vector cartoons with two white googly eyes standing in front of a painted landscape — they read as stickers. Checkpoints are a 4px brown stick and a 26px triangle. The two heart-stones — the emotional climax of the level — are a 66×10 grey rounded rect with a 4px double-circle 'heart' on top; at play scale a child cannot tell they are stand-here objects. The root gate is 5 green rounded rects + 6 ellipses and reads as a hedge or a cactus, not a gate. The frozen stream is a flat #c8eeff rounded rect with one white bar; the unfrozen water is a 55%-alpha blue rectangle with 4 sliding white dashes. The 128px soil tile was mirrored in both axes and now produces a visible kaleidoscope motif across every platform face (clearly legible in L01-0 and L01-3).

**Audio.** The only level with a music state change (meadow → restored, LevelScene.beginMeadowRestoration). Web Speech reads every tree name on the card, including wrong picks — the best teaching decision in the codebase. But: there is no ambience bed at all. A painted stream sits at x=760 and makes no sound; no birds, no grass, no wind. The 22-sfx synth carries the whole soundstage, and the pentatonic loop is the same three-mood score used for all 10 biomes. Waking a tree and lighting a torch share sfx vocabulary.

**Confusing or boring.** The helper dependency is invisible: the child is stopped by a puzzle whose real requirement is 'go back 250px and be kind to something', and that is communicated only by one Turkish/English sentence to an audience defined as pre-readers. The 7-step objective bar uses 💛 twice (steps 1 and 4) and 🌿/🌀 for two different puzzles — a 5-year-old cannot map that emoji row onto a place in the world. The gate check is a fuzzy positional test (|pcx−2420|<40 AND |feet−352|<26) with no floor decal marking the tolerance, so standing 45px off does nothing while the objective bar keeps insisting. And there is a dead 600px of pure walking between the bridge at 1825 and the gate at 2420 with nothing to look at, do, or learn.

**Proposed unique mechanic.** **Your friend is a step.** Once a creature is healed and happy, it becomes standable — a one-way (top-only) solid the Guardian can climb onto. Nothing else changes; no new button, no new icon. The level is then re-authored so the last third is only crossable that way: a ledge 60px above your jump apex, a gap you clear only by hopping off your friend's back mid-stride, and finally the twin heart-stones. Teachable with zero text because it is discovered by accident the first time the child jumps near the happy Mossling and lands on it — the friend gives a little squash-bounce and looks up. It makes the empathy loop mechanically load-bearing instead of optional, and it is different in kind from every other proposal in the band (L2 = environmental force, L3 = light economy). Implementation: a `oneWay` flag on Rect honoured in the y-collision pass, plus pushing the happy monster's mbox into solids() when it has been near-stationary for 0.2s.

**Proposed creature.** **Tomurcuk (Bud-lamb)** — a knee-high woolly grazer whose head is a closed flower bud on a long stem. Silhouette at 40px: a low, wide fleece cloud on four stick legs with one tall vertical stem — unmistakable against the Mossling's ear-tufted vertical roundness. It is never a threat and never chases: it stands asleep with its bud shut because the meadow is sick. It is restored not by sand but by proximity to a tree the child has woken — the bud opens into a flower head and it starts following the healed Mossling. By the finale a small line of them trails behind the pair: a visible, countable tally of the child's kindness walking with them to the oak.

**Proposed surprise.** At the twin heart-stones, the moment the Mossling settles onto its stone, the *dormant* Ancient Oak opens one eye — early, before the child has woken it — and looks at the creature, not at the player. The camera drifts 40px toward the oak and the music drops out for 1.2 seconds. A pre-reader reads it instantly: the big tree knows my friend. It reframes the whole level retroactively — the child was not rescuing a monster, they were returning someone home — and it makes the final recognition card a reunion rather than a quiz.

**Restoration transformation.** Today the restoration is a gold particle wash and a sprite crossfade in one screen. Make it travel: the moment the oak wakes, a bloom wave runs *right-to-left* down the full 3120px of grass edge at ~900px/s while the camera holds, so the child watches colour chase back over every place they walked. The stream at x=760 visibly thaws and starts flowing (animated, with water sound arriving for the first time). Every Bud-lamb in the trailing line opens at the instant the wave passes it. The foreground foliage clusters shift from their muted plate to a flowering variant. And the far background plate gains the mountain ridge of Level 2 emerging from the haze — the next region becomes visible because you fixed this one.

**Technical dependencies.** src/game/engine.ts (new one-way flag in the y-collision resolve — the only engine change in the band); src/game/LevelScene.ts solids() + the monster happy-state block + drawMeadowStory (heart-stone art) + updateMeadowRestoration (wave timing, camera hold); src/core/levels.ts level1 geometry re-author for the friend-step section; src/core/generator.ts Rect gains `oneWay?: boolean`; src/game/assets.ts + public/art/meadow (heart-stone decal, root-gate plate, flowering foreground variants, Bud-lamb sprite, stream flow strip); src/game/audio.ts (ambience bed + a 4th 'tender' mood for the eye-opening beat); docs/MEADOW_VISUAL_SYSTEM.md must be updated — it is the shipped contract. Tests: tests/scene.test.ts (one-way collision, helper-step gating), tests/core.test.ts (sand floor), tests/render.test.ts.

## Level 2 — Bölüm 2 · Zümrüt Zirveler

`biome: peaks — flat palette only. Grey-green soil #6b7a78 slabs with pure white tops, a 3-stop mint sky gradient, and two rows of fillEllipse 'hills'. No raster art of any kind. w=4400, 10 platforms.` · priority **P0** · effort **L** · risk **medium**

**Intended emotion.** Intended: exhilaration and exposure — thin air, height, a place that does not care whether you are small. Delivered: mild confusion, because nothing in the frame is high.

**Learning objective.** Four leaf-tier trees (çam, servi, huş, akçaağaç) — and this is the best-composed tree set in the game and it is completely wasted. Two conifers and two broadleaves is the natural teaching contrast: needle leaves vs flat leaves, and *why* — needles do not tear in mountain wind and do not freeze. The level should make the child feel that difference in their hands before the card ever asks the question.

**Current mechanic.** Nothing new. Six PUZZLE_FACTORY instances (freeze, thorn, grow, bridge, grow, thorn — so grow and thorn are each duplicated inside the level), six Mosslings including one 'flipper' that randomly reverses direction, and the shared thrower boss (hp 3, cage finisher, arcing shots on a 0.55s telegraph, arena wall at x=3422). The red 🔥 eye — an entirely new power — is introduced at x≈1170 with no intro line, no card, no cue.

**Repeated content.** freeze/grow/bridge are re-run from Level 1 within the first 2000px with identical factory geometry. Inside this level, grow appears twice and thorn twice. All six monsters are the same painted Mossling running the same aggro/patrol state machine; four of them sit within 800px of each other (2580 / 2856 / 3174 / 3380), so the last kilometre is monster-monster-monster-boss. The boss is the same 88×88 rounded rect with two eyes and three hp pips that levels 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 also use.

**Difficulty & pacing.** This is the churn point of the whole game — five escalations land simultaneously. gentle flips false (pits now cost a heart); a boss appears; a fifth power appears unannounced; monster count goes 1→6; and monsters[0]/[1] specify no spd/aggro so they fall back to engine defaults chase 130 / aggro 240 — meaning the very first creature of Level 2 is 1.6× faster and 1.3× more alert than the only creature of Level 1. Meanwhile the first intro hint in the file sits at x=2332: the opening 53% of the level, containing four puzzles, a brand-new power and two monsters, is taught with nothing at all, immediately after a tutorial that hand-held with six lines.

**Visual strengths.** Honestly: almost none. The pale mint-to-teal sky gradient is a pleasant colour choice and the white platform caps do suggest snow. The painted Guardian sprite still reads well against a light background because it carries a dark outline. That is the complete list.

**Visual weaknesses.** A mountain level with no mountains. Total platform elevation change is 38px across 4400px (y: 380,360,360,350,350,342,348,345,342,345) — this is a flat corridor wearing a mountain's name. The parallax is two rows of ellipses that read as soft mint bubbles; there is no ridge, no scale cue, no drop below the platforms, nothing to be *above*. Platforms are uniform grey rounded rects that read as concrete loading docks. Every tree is the same flat vector crown-shape library. The boss (L02-3) is a purple rounded rectangle with two white circles for eyes — after a hand-painted Ancient Oak, this is the moment a parent stops taking the game seriously. Ambient 'wisp' particles are not visible in any of the four captures.

**Audio.** Plays the *meadow* theme. main.ts calls setMusicMood('meadow') on every startLevel, so a mountain summit and a valley meadow are scored identically — and 'restored' only ever fires in Level 1, so this level has no music state change at all, including when the arena wall slams shut for the boss. No wind bed, no altitude cue, no thinning of the mix with height. The boss telegraph is a visual ring with no audio tell, which is exactly backwards for a 5-year-old.

**Confusing or boring.** It is Level 1 again with more of everything and none of the authored payoff, and the child feels that within ninety seconds. The objective bar reads 'Follow the glowing path →' for the entire 4400px and there is no glowing path — the one piece of directional text in the level is a lie. Six identical creatures with one identical solution turns the empathy loop into a chore queue: by the fourth Mossling the child is not being kind, they are clearing. Two grow puzzles and two thorn puzzles are the same object at different x. And nothing in the level acknowledges that the child has climbed anywhere.

**Proposed unique mechanic.** **Wind, and shelter from it.** Authored gust corridors (`wind: {x0,x1,ax,sign}[]` on LevelData) apply a horizontal acceleration to the Guardian while airborne — downwind jumps carry a third further, upwind jumps stall short. It is legible with zero text because the biome's ambient particles already exist: in a gust they streak horizontally, saplings bend, the Guardian's cape pulls sideways, and a bent windsock-sapling at the corridor mouth shows direction. The chain, and the reason this is a *puzzle* and not a hazard: waking a conifer (çam, servi) makes it grow into a windbreak that visibly kills the gust in its lee, opening a crossing that was impossible a moment before. So recognition stops being a quiz interrupt and becomes the key to the door. Implementable in ~25 lines: one vector add in the physics step, one boolean per tree, one lerp on gust strength.

**Proposed creature.** **Rüzgârkuşu (Gust-chick)** — a fledgling that cannot land. Silhouette at 40px: a narrow vertical spindle with two long trailing tail-ribbons and no visible legs, hovering unsteadily — nothing like the Mossling's wide, grounded, four-legged mass. It is not hostile; it is *caught*, tumbling along the ledge and knocking the Guardian off by accident, which is a far better lesson than an enemy. Calming sand drops the wind in a small bubble around it; healing lets it fold its wings and land. Once landed it perches on the nearest woken tree and stays there as a living wind-vane — its ribbons show the gust direction for the rest of the level. Restoring it hands the child a permanent readability tool.

**Proposed surprise.** At the summit, the wind stops. All at once: the streaming particles fall straight down, the mix drops to almost nothing, and the camera pulls back to show the whole Meadow of Level 1 laid out far below in the far plate — with the Ancient Oak the child woke visible as a single gold point in it. Then the boss reveals itself not as a monster on a ledge but as the thing that has been holding the storm off the valley, and has been doing it alone for too long. It reframes the boss encounter as relieving someone rather than beating them, and it is the first time in the game the world says: the place you fixed is still there, and you can see it from here.

**Restoration transformation.** When the storm-keeper is calmed, the change is structural and permanent for the rest of the level: the four woken trees close into a continuous windbreak ridgeline; every gust corridor drops to a drift and its particles slow visibly; the pure-white platform caps bloom into alpine flowers (a soil/grass palette swap on a left-to-right wave); the haze that has been flattening the sky lifts and the actual peak silhouettes — hidden behind the storm the whole time — resolve into the parallax for the first time, so the child finally sees the mountain they have been standing on. The Gust-chicks unperch and fly in a loose spiral around the summit.

**Technical dependencies.** src/core/generator.ts (LevelData gains `wind`); src/game/LevelScene.ts update() physics step + a wind-vane/gust renderer + tree-awake → windbreak state; src/core/levels.ts level2 re-authored for real verticality (the flat 38px profile must become a climb) and intro lines added before x=1170 for the red eye; src/core/biomes.ts peaks palette + a new ridge-silhouette parallax band; src/game/assets.ts + public/art/peaks (far ridge plate, storm-haze overlay, rock/snow soil tile, Gust-chick sprite, storm-keeper boss sprite); src/game/audio.ts (wind bed, a 'storm'/'summit' mood pair — the 3-mood score must grow); src/core/i18n.ts (new intro + objective strings; the fixed 3-step ['→','🏖️','✨'] bar in LevelScene.updateObjective must become per-level). Tests: tests/scene.test.ts (wind application, windbreak gating), tests/core.test.ts (monster default-speed regression), tests/render.test.ts.

## Level 3 — Bölüm 3 · Kristal Mağaralar

`biome: cave — flat palette with dark:true. Purple-grey slabs, a navy sky gradient, and a 62%-opacity 0x0a0718 overlay with 150px feathered radial punches at the player and at lit torches. w=4100, 10 platforms, deathY 760.` · priority **P0** · effort **L** · risk **high**

**Intended emotion.** Intended: the specific, delicious fear of a dark place, resolved into safety by light you made yourself. Delivered: mild dimness. The child is never actually in the dark, so they are never actually relieved.

**Learning objective.** Three leaf-tier trees — and this is the band's real integrity problem. Söğüt (willow, riverbank), zeytin (olive, Mediterranean hillside) and ginkgo are taught *inside a cave*. A game whose whole promise is 'learn trees and where they live' is currently teaching a false habitat association to five-year-olds. Two of the three (ginkgo, zeytin) also share crown:'oval', so in-world they are the same blob in two colours. The objective should be light and shelter: which plants can live where the sun barely reaches, and what does a plant do when it only gets one shaft of light a day.

**Current mechanic.** Introduces the purple 🟣 eye twice over (rock → shrink a blocking boulder; mush → grow a bounce pad, BOUNCE=1000 vs JUMP_V=720) and reuses red 🔥 for torches. Five Mosslings up to spd 158 including one flipper. Shared thrower boss with the **shrink** finisher — scale multiplies by 0.62 per cage, so the boss visibly ends the fight tiny and harmless. That is the single best boss beat in the game and nothing in the level, the UI or the docs draws attention to it. Seven intro lines, the most in the game and the only well-taught level in the band.

**Repeated content.** Three puzzle types, each duplicated verbatim: rock at 430 and 2342, torch at 1700 and 2678, mush at 560 and 2960 — the same PUZZLE_FACTORY object at a different x, with nothing added the second time. Monsters are the Mossling again, with the same numbers as Level 2's back half. The boss is Level 2's rectangle with a purple tint and a different finisher. The bounce pads exist in L6 and L8 too. Net new content in Level 3 is: the colour of the overlay.

**Difficulty & pacing.** Better taught than Level 2 (7 intros vs 5, and they land *before* each new idea rather than after), but it stacks bounce physics on top of a new power on top of five monsters ramping to spd 158. deathY 760 against platforms at 330–400 gives a generous ~360px fall grace, which is right. Both bounce pads launch into empty sky with nothing above them — a 1000-velocity launch that rewards the child with a view of the ceiling that does not exist. The shrink boss is the gentlest boss in the game and should be the *last* one a child meets, not the second.

**Visual strengths.** The soft-edged radial lantern (GLOW ramp [0,1],[0.6,0.88],[1,0] rather than a hard spotlight) is a genuinely tasteful piece of rendering. The purple-navy palette is atmospheric. The shrink finisher animates well. The torch flame — two stacked ellipses with a sine flicker — is the most charming vector object in the codebase.

**Visual weaknesses.** It is not a cave. There is no ceiling, no walls, no enclosure, and — despite the level being named 'Kristal Mağaralar' — not one crystal anywhere in four captures. The sky gradient and the parallax hill ellipses are still drawn and are clearly visible *through* the rock, so the child is looking at outdoor hills from inside a mountain. The 62% overlay leaves the entire layout readable (in L03-1 every platform, tree, flag and monster is legible), which means the torches light a level that was never dark: the level's signature mechanic solves a problem the player does not have. Platforms are the same rounded slabs in purple. The mush pad is a pink ellipse with four white dots. Trees are the same vector library, unlit, floating in a cave.

**Audio.** Plays the *meadow* theme, in a cave. This is the worst audio mismatch in the game: no reverb, no drips, no low drone, no change when the darkness closes in and no change when a torch relights. sfx('burn') for lighting a torch is the same cue as burning a thorn wall in Level 2, so the child's ear is told these are the same action when they are opposites (one destroys, one creates). The boss shrinking — the game's best visual joke — has no audio arc at all.

**Confusing or boring.** The torches are a ritual with no consequence, and children notice that faster than adults do. Two rock puzzles and two mush puzzles are the same puzzle twice, 1900px apart, with nothing learned in between. The level's name promises crystals and delivers none, which is a small betrayal but a real one. And the botanical incoherence is not just cosmetic: a child who learns 'olive tree = cave' in Bölüm 3 has been actively mistaught by the one system the game exists to deliver.

**Proposed unique mechanic.** **A light economy: carry it or plant it.** First make the dark real (overlay to ~0.9 outside the lantern, with a brightness floor tuned for a phone in daylight). Then give the Guardian *glow-seeds*, picked up by touch and planted with the same ✨ button: a planted seed becomes a permanent light-mushroom, but a carried seed is a lantern you keep. The catch, learned in one screen with no words: monsters walk toward light, not toward you. So while you carry it, they come to you; the moment you plant it, they trundle to the mushroom and leave the ledge you needed. With three seeds and four dark rooms the child must plan placement, retrieve, and re-place — a genuine spatial resource decision, and the first time in the game that thinking ahead beats reacting. Distinct in kind from L1 (cooperation) and L2 (environmental force), and it retires the decorative torch by making light scarce and consequential.

**Proposed creature.** **Işıkgöz (Glimmer-newt)** — a blind cave salamander: long, low, four tiny legs, wide flat head, translucent pink, no eyes. At 40px it is a horizontal ribbon roughly 3:1 — the exact inverse of the Mossling's 0.75:1 vertical roundness, so the two can never be confused even in shadow. It is not hostile, it is *dazzled*: it thrashes blindly when light passes over it and knocks the Guardian off ledges by accident. The existing loop still works and now means something — calming sand dulls the glare, healing lets it settle — and when it becomes happy **its skin lights up**, giving the child a free moving lantern that follows them. Restoring a creature literally solves the level's scarcity, which is the strongest possible argument the game can make for its own thesis. A deliberate escalation of Level 1's companion (there, your friend was a step; here, your friend is the light) built on a different underlying system.

**Proposed surprise.** The crystals are not crystals. Following the deepest passage, the child finds the glittering formations resolve — same bark texture, same silhouette — into the *fossilised roots of the Ancient Oak from Level 1*, reaching all the way down through the mountain. And the three trees are not growing underground at all: the passage opens into an obruk, a collapsed-ceiling sinkhole, where one shaft of daylight falls into a hidden green garden with an underground river running through it. That single move fixes the habitat lie (the willow really is on a riverbank, under real sky), pays off the level's name, and connects Bölüm 3 back to Bölüm 1 through the ground itself — the child understands that everything they have healed is one organism.

**Restoration transformation.** The boss is re-read as a lost stone-golem that has been hoarding every glow-seed in the cave because it is afraid of the dark — which is exactly what the child has spent the level being. When it is calmed it opens its hands and releases all of it at once: light climbs the walls, the fossil roots ignite along their whole length, the underground river reflects for the first time and throws moving caustics across the ceiling, and the darkness overlay drops permanently from ~0.9 to ~0.15 for the rest of the level, so the walk back out is through a cave the child can finally see — the same corridors, now legible. Every planted mushroom stays lit. The golem follows a few steps behind, in the light.

**Technical dependencies.** src/game/LevelScene.ts draw() darkness pass (opacity curve + brightness floor + per-seed radials + a real ceiling/wall layer that occludes the sky gradient and hill ellipses), monster AI (light-seeking target), doUse() (plant/pick-up), a new carried-item slot; src/core/levels.ts level3 re-authored for verticality and the obruk reveal, and the region tree set reframed so habitat is honest; src/core/world.ts magara region (treeSet + clueTier); src/core/biomes.ts cave palette + occluding cave-interior bands; src/core/generator.ts (LevelData gains seed/light placements); src/game/assets.ts + public/art/cave (ceiling and wall plates, root-crystal formation, daylight shaft, Glimmer-newt sprite, golem sprite); src/game/audio.ts (cave reverb bed, drips, a distinct 'light' cue split from sfx('burn'), a 4th/5th mood); src/core/i18n.ts. Tests: tests/scene.test.ts (seed carry/plant, light-seeking AI, darkness state), tests/render.test.ts (overlay floor), tests/core.test.ts.

## Level 4 — Bölüm 4 · Kestane Korusu

`biome: forest — an amber/autumn palette (skyTop #fbe8c8 → grass #b5772f). Every colour in the level sits inside one 40° hue wedge; the only saturated element on screen is a green tree blob.` · priority **P0** · effort **L** · risk **medium**

**Intended emotion.** Intended (per name + hint 'üç yeni ağaç seni bekliyor'): the warm, crunchy delight of an autumn grove, and the first step into unknown country after the hand-authored trio. Actually delivered: mild disorientation — the child leaves Level 3's crafted cave and lands in a beige void with one line of text and 4,196 px of identical ground.

**Learning objective.** Currently nominal: 'name kestane, kayın, kavak from a leaf'. Proposed: seed dispersal — heavy seeds fall straight down and land in armour (kestane, kayın = both Kayıngiller), light seeds fly away on the wind (kavak, Söğütgiller). The mechanic and the botany become the same lesson.

**Current mechanic.** makeSection() recipe: tier 2, puzzles grow → bridge → thorn, cageEye green, thrower boss, shrink=false/cage finisher. 8 platforms, 3 calm (tree+checkpoint) alternating with 3 puzzle (puzzle+mossling), then a 2-platform boss arena. Zero mechanics that do not already exist in Levels 1–3.

**Repeated content.** Total. (1) Platform layout is SECTION_RHYTHM verbatim — same six widths as Levels 5–10; y = 370 − (floor(i/2)%3)*6, i.e. 12 px of total elevation change across the whole level, and every gap is exactly 152 px. (2) All 3 puzzle types are re-runs of Level 1/2 (grow, bridge, thorn) with no variation in parameters. (3) All 3 monsters are the same Mossling sprite at identical spd 102 / aggro 189. (4) The boss is the same purple rounded-rect-with-two-eyes as Levels 2 and 3, differing only in cageEye colour. (5) Objective bar is the shared 3-step ['→','🏖️','✨'] with 'Follow the glowing path'. (6) The intro is 1 raw Turkish string literal in the recipe with no i18n key — EN and DE children are shown Turkish.

**Difficulty & pacing.** Flat and low, with one unfair edge. There is no in-level ramp: jump 1 and jump 7 are the identical 152 px gap. Every gap is a bottomless pit (platform tops ~370, deathY 760) so the sole failure mode is instant heart loss. Resource pressure is zero: sandMax 8 for 3 monsters + boss, checkpoints refill, and the boss auto-refills sand every 1.4 s when empty. Critically, the empathy loop — the entire thesis of the game — is OPTIONAL here: monsters can be tanked through on iframes or jumped over, and a healed monster in a generated level just stands still bobbing (the follow/cooperate behaviour is gated behind idx===0). Healing pays +1 heart and nothing else. Also: makeSection sets flip:true on monster #3 (random direction reversal) but gives it no visual tell whatsoever, so its one moment of variation reads as a random unfair hit.

**Visual strengths.** The amber palette is the only autumn in the ten-level world, so it does read as a distinct season at a glance. The vector tree work has genuine craft under the flatness — forked branches, root flares, a two-tone crown with sheen dabs. The pulsing coloured eye-mark on an unsolved puzzle is a clean, wordless affordance. Checkpoint flags flip from bone to green when passed — a good silent progress signal.

**Visual weaknesses.** Sky, hills, soil and 'grass' are all the same orange family, so the platform edge, the horizon and the background merge into one wash — see L04-0/1/2 where the ground plane is nearly invisible. The platform 'grass' strip is brown (#b5772f), so platforms read as floating dirt slabs with a dashed yellow border rather than ground. The entire parallax is two rows of plain ellipses (hillsFar, hillsMid) — compare Meadow's painted far plate + treeline + three foreground clusters. There is no midground, no foreground layer, nothing occluding the player. Trees are 2-tone circle stacks with two white dots for eyes. The boss is literally fillRoundedRect + two circles. biomes.ts declares ambientA/ambientB/ambientShape:'leaf' for this biome — that data is never read by any renderer, so the advertised falling leaves do not exist.

**Audio.** main.ts startLevel() hard-sets setMusicMood('meadow') for every level index, so Level 4 plays the identical exploration pentatonic loop from spawn through the boss to completion — no arrival cue, no boss music, no restoration music ('restored' only ever fires inside beginMeadowRestoration, idx===0). There is no ambient bed of any kind — no wind, no birds, no leaf-litter footsteps; the 'distant leaf hush' noise burst every 8th note is biome-agnostic. sfx are the shared 22-item set; puzzle activation reuses 'grow'/'wake'/'burn' exactly as in Level 1. speak() correctly reads the recognition card, which is the one genuinely good audio-accessibility win.

**Confusing or boring.** Brutally: this is where a 5-year-old quits. (1) The objective bar says 'Follow the glowing path' — there is no glowing path rendered anywhere; the only glowing things are the sleeping-tree lanterns and the puzzle eye-marks. The child is told to follow something that does not exist. (2) The level's actual goal is never communicated — the child runs right for 4,196 px on faith. (3) One intro hint fires at x=60 for 2.6 s, in Turkish regardless of language, and never repeats; Levels 1–3 had 6–7. So the level with the LEAST support is the first one without hand-authoring. (4) The captured screenshots L04-0..3 show a Meadow-only string leaking over this level — 'The roots are waiting for a friend's heart. Go back to the frightened creature!' — which is meaningless here (tracked as B-01). (5) The recognition question actively mis-teaches: art.ts LEAF_PATH contains only 8 species paths (meşe, çam, söğüt, kestane, toros sediri, elma, palmiye, ladin) and falls back to the OAK path for everything else — so kayın and kavak are both drawn as oak leaves, differing only in a subtle green hue, while the game's own TREES data tells the child that kavak is 'üçgen yapraklı' (triangle-leaved). The child is asked to identify a leaf that is drawn wrong.

**Proposed unique mechanic.** PUSH — the level where objects gain weight. Spiky green chestnut husks hang under branches; standing beneath one and using 🌿 drops it. A landed husk is a solid the child can also shove by walking into it (it visibly rocks on contact, so a 5-year-old discovers push within one second, no text). Husks are used three ways that ramp: (a) drop one into a gap as a stepping stone — and there are 3 husks over a gap needing 2, so the child authors their own solution for the first time; (b) push a husk under a high ledge to gain height; (c) push a husk onto a leaf-drift to soften a landing. Distinct from Level 5 (a continuous world force) and Level 6 (a cognitive matching verb) because this one is discrete, physical and object-scale. Engine cost is small: a rect with a fall-tween plus a horizontal nudge resolved in the existing player-collision step.

**Proposed creature.** Dikenkabuk (the Curled One) — a chestnut-hedgehog the size of a Mossling, rolled into a spiky ball and visually indistinguishable from a husk until you are close. Silhouette at 40 px: a spiked circle, unmistakably different from the Mossling's round body with two ears and a curled tail. It is not angry and cannot be sanded — it is already hiding. It is restored by being pushed gently onto a soft leaf-drift, where it uncurls, blinks, and stands up. Non-violent by construction: the resolution is giving something a soft place to land.

**Proposed surprise.** The child spends the first third of the level pushing husks. Then they push one and it BLINKS. The 'husk' was alive the whole time — which retroactively recolours every husk they already shoved and every husk still ahead. After uncurling it walks with you and helps push the one husk that is too heavy for one pair of hands, escalating Meadow's one-off cooperation finale into a moment-to-moment verb. A story beat and a systems beat in the same reveal.

**Restoration transformation.** Today: nothing. completeLevel() fires particles and a DOM card; the world is unchanged. Proposed, and level-wide visible: on restoration the leaf litter LIFTS — a wave of leaves runs left→right along the full 4,196 px and settles back up into the canopies, while the biome palette lerps over ~3 s from amber to green-gold (sky #fbe8c8→#dff1d8, grass #b5772f→#6fbf5c). The bare trunks the child walked past sprout crowns behind them; every husk they pushed cracks open and a green sprout stands up in it; the Dikenkabuk uncurls fully and a second one comes out of the drift to meet it. Camera pulls back so the child sees the level they just crossed, re-coloured.

**Technical dependencies.** New 'husk' entry in PUZZLE_FACTORY + a draw case + a solids() case; a pushable-solid resolution inside the proven player-collision step (~40 lines, must not perturb CONFIG.physics); a new monster type 'curled' with its own draw branch and a push-onto-drift resolution replacing sandHit; a per-level objective descriptor — updateObjective() currently hard-codes idx===0 vs 'everything else', so this is a shared prerequisite with Levels 5 and 6; i18n keys for intros (recipes currently carry raw TR literals); a runtime-interpolated BiomePalette for the restoration lerp; correct LEAF_PATH entries for kayın and kavak (kavak must be triangular) before the recognition card is honest; a 4th music mood plus a restoration stinger.

## Level 5 — Bölüm 5 · Toros Yaylası

`biome: toros — pale high-plateau blue/white sky with mint hill ellipses and olive-grey ground. Declares ambientShape:'snow'; no snow is ever drawn.` · priority **P0** · effort **XL** · risk **high**

**Intended emotion.** Intended: thin air, altitude, exposure, the awe of thousand-year cedars — the hint literally promises 'Yayla rüzgarı sert' (the highland wind is harsh). Delivered: an empty pale-grey screen with no wind, no snow, no altitude and no cedars that look like cedars. The level makes a promise in its first line of text and then breaks it for four thousand pixels.

**Learning objective.** Currently nominal: 'name toros sediri, ardıç, sekoya from BARK' — this region is where the clue tier steps up from leaf to bark. Proposed: conifers are not one tree — you tell a cedar from a juniper from a sequoia by its CONE and its BARK, not by its leaf. This is exactly the tier the region already claims, and exactly what is currently broken.

**Current mechanic.** makeSection() recipe: tier 3, puzzles freeze → rock → bridge, cageEye blue, thrower boss with the shrink finisher. Identical structure to Level 4 with 6 px more gap. The one new note in the whole level is that the boss shrinks instead of being caged — a 0.62 scale multiplier the child will not read as a different idea.

**Repeated content.** This is the worst offender in the band: Level 5 and Level 6 are the SAME LEVEL. Both are tier 3, both come out of makeSection at w=4244 with 8 platforms, the same six widths, the same 158 px gaps, the same 3 checkpoints, the same 3 Mosslings at spd 112 / aggro 201, the same sandMax 8. The only differences a player can perceive are the palette, the tree names and two of three puzzle types. Beyond that: freeze and bridge are Level 1 re-runs, rock is a Level 3 re-run, the boss is the Level 2/3/4 rounded rect, the objective bar is the shared 3-step 'Follow the glowing path', and the single intro is again a raw untranslated Turkish literal.

**Difficulty & pacing.** Nominally the difficulty step from Level 4 is monsters +10 spd and +12 aggro — imperceptible. Layout difficulty is identical (same flat line, same repeated gap, same bottomless pits). The shrink finisher makes the boss progressively EASIER to avoid as its hitbox shrinks, so the boss's difficulty curve runs backwards. Sand economy is again non-binding (checkpoint refill + boss auto-refill). Net: the fifth level of ten is no harder than the fourth, and its only new idea makes its climax softer.

**Visual strengths.** The pale, desaturated plateau does communicate 'high, cold, empty' — the one biome whose palette is genuinely on-brief for its concept. The conifer crown (three stacked triangles) is a clean, instantly readable silhouette and the strongest tree shape in the vector set. Contrast between the dark trunk and the pale sky is the best figure/ground relationship in the generated band.

**Visual weaknesses.** Saturation range is so narrow that the whole screen reads as unfinished grey-blue (L05-0..3). The mint hill ellipses at the bottom are LIGHTER than the sky above them, which inverts the depth cue — the background appears to come forward. Declared snow motes are never rendered (ambientA/ambientB/ambientShape are dead data across the entire codebase). The 'thousand-year cedar' is the same three flat triangles used for çam, servi, ardıç, sekoya and ladin — with no needle texture, no cones, no bark, and near-identical greens (#3f7a5c vs #5c8a6e vs #4f8a52), so all three of this region's trees are visually the same object. There is no vertical drama in a level named after mountains: 12 px of elevation change total.

**Audio.** Same as the rest of the band — startLevel() forces the 'meadow' mood, so a highland plateau is scored with meadow music. A wind level with no wind sound is the most conspicuous audio hole in the game: the biome name, the hint text and the proposed mechanic all call for a filtered-noise bed with gust swells, and audio.ts already has a noise() generator that could supply it for a few lines. No boss cue, no altitude cue, no snow/ice sfx despite the freeze puzzle being present.

**Confusing or boring.** (1) The bark recognition tier debuts here and it is mathematically unanswerable. drawBarkSwatch() groups 'çam','servi','toros sediri','ardıç','ladin' into ONE branch — so the bark clue for toros sediri and the bark clue for ardıç are byte-identical images. The region pool is exactly {toros sediri, ardıç, sekoya}, so pick3 always puts both conifers on screen. When the answer is one of them, the child cannot distinguish it from a wrong answer by any means other than luck. The choice cards do not save it either: getChoiceArt draws leaf + bark, and ardıç and sekoya both fall back to the OAK leaf path. The learning loop — the reason this game exists — is broken in this level. (2) The level and Level 6 are indistinguishable in structure, so the child's fifth and sixth hours are the same hour twice. (3) 'Follow the glowing path' again, with no path. (4) The intro promises harsh wind that does not exist — the game lies to the player in its only line of teaching. (5) The screenshots L05-0..2 again carry the leaked Meadow string about roots and a frightened creature (B-01).

**Proposed unique mechanic.** THE WIND — the level where the world pushes back. Continuous horizontal force zones on a slow, telegraphed ~4 s cycle: the ambient motes stream visibly in the gust direction (finally consuming BIOME.ambientA/B/ambientShape), every tree crown and grass tuft tilts the same way, and a low whoosh precedes each reversal, so a 5-year-old learns the rhythm by watching, never by reading. Downwind the child's jump carries much further; upwind they must wait. Chain (the puzzle-chaining ramp the band needs): the existing freeze puzzle gains a second life — freezing the plateau stream builds a WINDBREAK wall, and the segment in its lee goes still, which is the only way to stand steady long enough to work the rock puzzle behind it. Two systems interacting instead of seven isolated puzzles. Genuinely different from Level 4 (discrete object-scale push) and Level 6 (cognitive matching): this one is continuous and world-scale.

**Proposed creature.** Yelkuzu (the Wind Lamb) — a small highland creature of cloud-wool that is being blown helplessly along the plateau and cannot stop running. Silhouette at 40 px: a wide horizontal woolly blob on four thin legs with a trailing wisp of wool — nothing like the Mossling's vertical round body with ears and tail. It is not angry; it is lost in the wind. Restoration is not sand: the child guides it into shelter — behind the frozen windbreak or into the lee of a cedar — where it finally stops, folds its legs and sleeps. The cedar's real gift (a dense wall against the wind) becomes the mechanic that saves it.

**Proposed surprise.** The wind is not weather. Following the gusts upwind to their source, the child finds that the boss is not throwing anything at all — the giant is SHIVERING on the highest ledge, and every gust in the level has been its breath. Soothing it stops the wind everywhere at once and the entire plateau goes still and silent. A systems beat: the level's core mechanic turns out to have had an author, and that author was cold and frightened rather than hostile — which is the game's thesis stated in mechanics instead of text.

**Restoration transformation.** Today: nothing changes. Proposed, level-wide and unmissable: the wind stops. Every mote in the air drops straight down; every bent tree, grass tuft and banner springs upright in one visible beat — a silhouette change across the whole screen. The frozen windbreak melts and runs, filling the dry channel that has been empty along the level's base with a moving stream. Cedar saplings pop up in the lee of every rock the child shrank. The washed-out sky deepens and gains its first real cloud shadows. Audio: the constant wind bed cuts out — silence is the reward, and it will be the loudest moment in the game.

**Technical dependencies.** windZones data on LevelData + a force term in the player update (must be bounded and provably unable to cause an unavoidable pit death — needs a dedicated test, and assistFactors should scale wind strength down after repeated deaths); a streaming mote field in the drawn layer, consuming the currently-dead BIOME.ambient* fields; a tilt parameter threaded through drawTree/grass; a windbreak flag on the freeze interact consumed by the wind solver; three genuinely distinct bark swatches in drawBarkSwatch for toros sediri / ardıç / sekoya plus cone motifs (cedar barrel cones upright on the branch, juniper blue berries, sequoia tiny eggs) — the wind can blow cones off the trees for the child to collect and match, tying the learning to the mechanic; a new 'shiver' boss idle state and boss art; a wind noise bed plus a highland music mood and a silence beat; the shared per-level objective descriptor from the Level 4 row.

## Level 6 — Bölüm 6 · Meyve Bahçesi

`biome: orchard — cream-to-peach sky, honey light, green grass. Declares ambientShape:'petal'; no petal is ever drawn.` · priority **P1** · effort **L** · risk **medium**

**Intended emotion.** Intended: an abundant, sweet, sun-warmed orchard, plus the delicious unease of the hint 'Bahçede bir Taklitçi saklanıyor' (a Mimic is hiding in the garden). Delivered: the warmest sky in the game with dead grey trees standing in it and a green box for a Mimic. The level has the best premise of the three and the widest gap between premise and screen.

**Learning objective.** Currently nominal: 'name elma, kiraz, ceviz from bark'. Proposed: a tree's flower and its fruit are the same thing at two ages — and trees in the same family share a flower. Elma and kiraz are both Gülgiller (five-petalled), ceviz is Cevizgiller (no showy flower at all). So the hard pair is precisely the pair that teaches the family, and the journal's family-star system finally gets an expression in play instead of only in a menu.

**Current mechanic.** makeSection() recipe: tier 3, puzzles grow → mush → bridge, cageEye green, MIMIC boss with the cage finisher. The mimic is the only mechanically distinct boss in the entire game — recognition is the finisher, via showMimicQuestion at a forced ≥bark tier — and it is presented in exactly the same rounded rectangle as the thrower with four flat green circles balanced on top.

**Repeated content.** Structurally identical to Level 5 — same makeSection tier 3 output: w=4244, 8 platforms, same six widths, same 158 px gaps, same 3 checkpoints, same 3 Mosslings at spd 112 / aggro 201, same sandMax 8. Two of three puzzles (grow, bridge) are Level 1 re-runs; mush is a Level 3 re-run. Shared 3-step objective bar, shared 'Follow the glowing path', one raw untranslated Turkish intro. The only non-repeated element in the level is the mimic's question card — which is a DOM overlay, so the playfield itself contains nothing the child has not already played three times.

**Difficulty & pacing.** Identical to Level 5's numbers, meaning the sixth of ten levels is exactly as hard as the fourth. The mimic should raise the ceiling — it demands recognition under pressure — but it is undercut: the mimic tier is bark, and elma's bark falls through drawBarkSwatch to the anonymous grey default used by roughly a dozen species, so one of the three answers is a texture that means nothing. A wrong answer costs only a small shake and 'hmm', so the boss can be brute-forced by tapping cards, which is exactly the dark-pattern-free-but-learning-free outcome to avoid. Meanwhile the three Mosslings are as skippable as everywhere else in the band.

**Visual strengths.** The peach/cream sky is the most beautiful palette in the ten-biome set and the warmest light in the game. The mimic's green body against that peach sky is the strongest figure/ground contrast of any boss — the shape is bad but its placement in the colour field is right. The grass strip finally has a real green (#7cc257) so platforms read as ground here rather than as dirt slabs, which is not true in Level 4.

**Visual weaknesses.** Sleeping trees are force-rendered in grey-blue (#718b82/#526f69) regardless of species, so in the one biome whose entire identity is blossom, every tree the child meets looks ashen and dead — the worst palette clash in the game (see L06-0/1/2). There is no blossom, no petal, no fruit, no basket, no fence, no orchard row anywhere in the level; 'orchard' exists only as a sky gradient. The declared petal ambient is dead data. The mimic's 'leafy disguise' is four flat circles on a rectangle's top edge (L06-3), which does not read as a tree at any distance, so the fiction 'it disguised itself as a tree' is never actually seen — the reveal has nothing to reveal. Same two-ellipse parallax, no midground, no foreground.

**Audio.** Same forced 'meadow' mood; the orchard is scored as a meadow. The mimic — the game's most interesting boss — gets no musical identity, no tell, no sting on the reveal, and shares the ordinary 'cage' resolution sfx. There is no wind-through-blossom bed, no bee hum, no fruit-drop sfx. speak() reading the mimic question is good; a light, low, slightly-wrong pitch-shifted variant of the tree-wake motif for the mimic would carry the whole idea with about six lines of audio code.

**Confusing or boring.** (1) The Mimic is the best idea in the game and it is invisible: nothing about the green box says 'tree', so a 5-year-old is asked to find the real tree among cards while the fake tree on screen looks like a box. (2) The level is Level 5 again with a different sky. (3) Sleeping trees rendered grey in a blossom orchard actively contradicts the biome's promise. (4) 'Follow the glowing path' for the third level running, with no path. (5) One Turkish-only intro that mentions the Taklitçi and then never mentions it again for 4,000 px until the arena. (6) The bark tier is again half-broken (elma = generic default swatch), so the mimic's climactic question can be answered without knowing anything about apples. (7) Wrong answers are free and the card re-opens, so guessing dominates learning at the exact moment the game most wants the child to look carefully.

**Proposed unique mechanic.** GRAFTING — the level where the learning loop IS the traversal. Blossom clusters drift on the wind; the child carries exactly ONE at a time, visibly in hand (a strong, unmissable state for a five-year-old), and brings it to the bare branch whose fruit it belongs to. A correct pairing makes that branch burst into flower AND become a solid platform, so the orchard cannot be crossed without matching blossom to tree. A wrong pairing does nothing punitive — the blossom simply floats gently back. Difficulty comes from the carry constraint and backtracking, not dexterity, which is the right axis for ages 5–8. This is categorically different from Level 4 (physical push) and Level 5 (world force): it is the first cognitive verb, and it sets up the Mimic boss as the same verb at boss scale rather than as an unrelated quiz.

**Proposed creature.** Şekerkuş (the Sugarbird) — a plump, blossom-crowned bird that has eaten fermented windfall fruit and is dizzy: it staggers, loops, and knocks blossoms off branches, which is diegetically WHY the orchard's blossoms are scattered and the child has to carry them. Silhouette at 40 px: a wide teardrop body, one long thin beak, two swept tail feathers, in a perch crouch — unmistakably not a Mossling. Non-violent and non-sandable: it is dizzy, not hostile. It is restored by the child HOLDING STILL for about 1.5 s with an arm out until it lands — an inversion of every other creature interaction in the game and a genuine stillness beat for an over-stimulated child. Once landed it becomes a blossom-fetcher, retrieving one from a branch the child cannot reach.

**Proposed surprise.** The Mimic's true nature. The child has spent the whole level matching blossom to fruit, so they arrive at the arena fluent in one rule. In the arena stand four beautiful trees, and one of them offers the child a blossom — but its blossom and its fruit do not match: an apple crown on a cherry trunk. The reveal is that the Mimic is not evil; it is a young tree that never learned what it is, copying whoever stands nearby. When the child names it correctly, it stops copying, settles, and grows its own true crown on screen. Story beat and systems beat in one, and the thematic capstone of the recognition band's midpoint.

**Restoration transformation.** Today: nothing — particles and a DOM card. Proposed: the orchard runs a whole year in four seconds, left to right, in time with the music. Pass one, a blossom wave travels the full 4,244 px and every branch the child grafted flowers. Pass two, the petals fall as pink snow (the declared 'petal' ambient, finally alive) and fruit visibly swells on the branches behind it. The ground crossfades from bare soil to grass strewn with fallen fruit; every grey sleeping tree the child woke is now in full colour. The Mimic, newly named, stands at the far end wearing the crown of its true species — the one tree the child can point at and say what it is.

**Technical dependencies.** A single-slot carry state on the player plus a paired pickup/dropoff interact type; branch activation should reuse the existing grow-leaves solids() pattern so the collision path stays proven; real mimic art — an actual tree silhouette wearing a wrong crown, plus the settle-into-its-species transformation (the mimic already has its own code path in doUse/resolveMimicAnswer, so this is mostly drawing); a stillness check for the Sugarbird landing (player |vx| ≈ 0 for N seconds — trivial); a petal ambient field consuming BIOME.ambient*; a distinct elma bark swatch and blossom motifs per species (5-petal for Gülgiller, catkin for ceviz) so the family lesson is visible; the shared per-level objective descriptor and i18n'd intro keys from the Level 4 row; an orchard music mood plus a mimic tell and a blossom-wave stinger.
